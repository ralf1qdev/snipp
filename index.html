FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Snipp free?

Yes. Snipp is free to download and use, with no account, ads or trial period.

Windows shows "Windows protected your PC" when I run the installer.

That's Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, which warns about new downloads that haven't built up a reputation yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

PrtSc opens the Windows Snipping Tool instead of Snipp.

On Windows 11, PrtSc is assigned to the built-in tool by default. Turn off Settings › Accessibility › Keyboard › Use the Print screen key to open screen capture, or choose a different shortcut in Snipp's settings. Snipp tells you when it detects this.

Does Snipp upload my screenshots anywhere?

No. Snipp never connects to the internet. Snips stay on your PC — on the clipboard, in the folder you choose, and in your local history (which you can turn off or clear in Settings). Text recognition also runs entirely offline.

Where are my snips stored?

Saved snips go to Pictures\Snipp by default (changeable in Settings). Recent-snip history lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Snipp\History and keeps the last 50 by default.

Do I need administrator rights?

No. Snipp installs just for your user account, so there's no UAC prompt.

How do I uninstall it?

Open Settings › Apps, find Snipp and choose Uninstall. Your saved screenshots are kept; you can choose whether to remove your settings too.

What are the system requirements?

Windows 10 or Windows 11 (64- or 32-bit). Snipp uses the .NET Framework that's already part of Windows, so there's nothing else to install.

About

Small on purpose.

Snipp started from a simple frustration: taking a screenshot, pointing at something and blurring a password shouldn't require a heavyweight app, an account or a cloud upload.

So Snipp is native, starts instantly and weighs about half a megabyte. It does the everyday things — capture, annotate, redact, copy text — really well, and stays out of your way the rest of the time.
